Fixing the T265→Robot Direction Bug

Why the arm moved sideways instead of forward, how the true axis mapping and base calibration were derived from real reference data instead of guesswork, and what still needs attention.

Branch ur7e-dev6.0-testing Commit 72c8c51 Verified on nestest/episode_1.hdf5, Pick_and_place_the_bottle/episode_1.hdf5

Summary

  • Fixed  The recorded handheld motion was being interpreted along the wrong robot axes — a T265→robot axis remap (_T265_TO_ROBOT) was fitted from two real motion segments you identified in the video (home→pick, pick→place), instead of guessed.
  • Fixed  base_position/base_orientation were re-derived from a real UR pendant reading, correcting for a known 180° convention difference between the pendant's native frame and the robot's URDF frame.
  • Fixed  _PREFERRED_BRANCH updated to match the corrected calibration, so the arm reaches with the right elbow/shoulder configuration, not just the right end-point.
  • Open  A kinematic wrist singularity right at the grasp point causes a brief joint-angle jump — a separate, known issue, not fixed here.

01 The problem

The MuJoCo replay of recorded episodes reproduced motion of roughly the right scale, but the arm moved in the wrong direction — most visibly, a motion you knew should go forward came out as the arm swinging sideways, and the starting pose didn't match the real robot's confirmed home position.

02 How it was isolated

Each stage of the pipeline was checked against independent ground truth before moving to the next, so the bug couldn't hide behind a compounded error.

  • FK/URDF chain confirmed correct first
    A static render using the real pendant's joint-angle readout (no episode data, no transform chain) matched a real photo of the robot exactly. This ruled out the URDF, mesh chain, and MuJoCo model as the source of the bug.
  • IK / FK solve proven exact
    Replaying a full episode's solved joint angles through forward kinematics reproduced the original Cartesian targets to 0.3 mm RMS, with the best-fit rotation between target and replay measuring 0.03° — i.e. no rotation at all. The analytic IK solver and its _RZ180 module-frame correction were not the problem.
  • Coordinate-frame audit, line by line
    Every stage from raw T265 pose to the final IK target was traced through the actual code, not the comments. This found that transform_to_base_quat() adds the local T265 displacement directly onto base_position with no rotation applied — meaning a T265-frame direction and a robot-frame direction were being silently treated as the same thing.
  • Home pose calibrated against a real pendant reading
    You provided the UR pendant's Tool Position reading at the task's home pose. Applied directly it was off by 1.45 m — because the pendant reports position in UR's native Base frame, not the ROS/URDF base_link frame, and the two differ by a fixed 180° yaw. Corrected for that, the reading matched the real joint pose (via FK) to 1.7 mm / 0.42°.
  • Direction confirmed against two real motion segments
    You identified three points in the recorded video by frame number: home (A), bottle pick (B), bottle place (C), and described the real motion as A→B along one screen axis, B→C along the other. Fitting a rotation to match that reduced full-episode IK-unreachable frames from 41/207 to 2/207.

Note on the reachability metric: reachability/smoothness alone can't prove a calibration is correct — rotating an entire trajectory by an arbitrary fixed matrix often stays just as smooth and reachable. The number above is confirmation the fit holds up end-to-end, not the basis the fit was chosen on; the axis mapping itself was fitted directly to your two identified motion segments.

05 Known remaining issue — not fixed here

Right at the grasp point (episode frames ~73–80), the target pose sits close to a wrist singularity. The Cartesian target barely moves in this region, but the solved joint angles swing between a fully extended pose and a collapsed one — wrist_3 was observed wrapping almost a full 360° between two consecutive frames. This is a property of that specific pose relative to the robot's reach geometry, not a calibration error, and needs a different kind of fix (e.g. biasing the IK branch search away from singular configurations, or smoothing across the affected frames).
